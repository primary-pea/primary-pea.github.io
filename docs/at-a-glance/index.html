At a glance

The scaffold, source to result

Twelve public datasets go in; one guarded script turns them into a single country-year table; seven models read that table; every number that comes out is tagged with the script and the statistic that produced it. The diagram is the map. Click any box to open its section: what it is, what went in and in what shape, what the code does, what came out. The Method page explains why each choice was made.

The flow

Sources

Twelve datasets from eight organisations, plus the lookup that keys them. Each arrived as a pinned file with a provenance record, through a committed pull script, or from the team repository.

source · WHO via FAOSTAT · pinned parquetWHO anaemia estimates, women 15–49The outcome series: modelled annual prevalence, 49 countries, 2000–2023, one column, read by every model.

WHO's 2025 edition of anaemia estimates (Global Health Observatory indicator 4552) comes from a Bayesian hierarchical model fitted to 412 surveys from 122 countries; FAOSTAT republishes it in the Suite of Food Security Indicators as item 21043. The scaffold reads the FAOSTAT copy because FAOSTAT area codes are pinned in the country lookup. The UNICEF workbook carries the same series; the assembly checks the two columns cell by cell (largest difference 0.0) and keeps the duplicate only for that check, so the panel has one anaemia series, not two.

In

inputs/faostat/data_FS_item21043_anaemia_women_15_49_pct.parquet: 5,616 rows × 14 columns, every country and year in the July 2026 release (20 KB), byte-identical copy with its SHA-256 in raw_copies.provenance.json.

Sample used

Rows with Element == "Value"; area code mapped to ISO3 through the lookup; the 49 countries, 2000–2025.

Out

One panel column, out_anaemia_wra_fs_pct: 1,176 filled cells (49 countries × 2000–2023), none missing.

Where it goes. The left-hand side of every regression: the dose ladder, both event studies, all 336 specifications, the held-out fit, the context ranking and the double-ML sidebar; the first of the four concern-score indicators; one of the four components of the women's index; the map and the small-multiple trends.

What to keep in mind. It is a modelled series. In a country-year without a survey the value is a covariate prediction (the WHO model uses the Socio-Demographic Index, meat supply and mean BMI), and within-country movement is partly the model's own smoothing (autocorrelation about 0.98). That is why the ladder carries a survey-anchored row and why the site says association, never effect.

Assembly

One script and one module turn the sources into a grid, or stop.

script 00 · 00_assemble_panel.pyOne guarded script builds the gridTwenty-nine inputs go in; a complete 49 × 26 grid with 174 columns comes out, or the script stops.

The logic, in order.

  1. Start from the full grid. The panel is created as every combination of the 49 ISO3 codes and the 26 years, so every source is a left join onto a fixed skeleton. A source can never add or remove a country-year.
  2. Load and tidy each source into three columns, iso3, year, value: years and values coerced to numbers, nulls dropped, only the 49 countries and 2000–2025 kept.
  3. Guard. Duplicate (country, year) keys stop the build with the source named ("resolve upstream, not with .first()"). A country with no values at all must be declared in config.EXPECTED_MISSING; an undeclared gap stops the build for the core sources and is logged for the context series. Every input path is registered the moment it is opened, so a missing file fails early with the environment variable to set.
  4. Join with validate="one_to_one", then record a readiness row: countries covered, first and last year, cells filled, countries without values.
  5. The registry reader runs after the ordinary sources and returns its 60 columns plus each country's first-adoption years; the build refuses any missing value in those columns (zero means no record, never unknown).
  6. The events table is assembled from the first-adoption years, the GFDx mandate years, the UNICEF sub-region and the terrain variables, one row per country.
  7. Grid check. check_panel_grid requires unique keys and exactly 49 × 26 rows, then the identical-series check compares the two anaemia columns.
  8. Hash and stamp. Every input and the panel itself are hashed with SHA-256 into MANIFEST.json; the first twelve hex digits of the panel's hash are the data version, afe63e46c26b. Change any input and the version changes.

Nothing is imputed. A missing value stays missing and is counted in the readiness table. The two exceptions are rules, not guesses: the survey flag is 0 where there was no survey, and a registry feature is 0 where the registry holds no record.

29 files, about 12 MB: 24 pinned under scaffold/inputs/ (workbooks, parquets, CSVs, the cluster rules, the lookup, provenance records) and 5 from the team repo (three registry files, the feature table, the income file).

module · gifna_direct.pyRegistry reader: tokens → clusters → featuresDeterministic features from the registry's controlled fields, with a dating rule and no language model.

Classification. Only the controlled columns are read (policy Topics; programme Theme, Topic, Target_Group, Micronutrient; mechanism Topics and Type). Each cell is split on | into tokens, and each token is matched, case-insensitively, against the 47 regular expressions in inputs/gifna_cluster_rules.csv. A rule names one of eleven clusters: anaemia, fortification, micronutrient, maternal, infant-and-young-child feeding, wasting and stunting, infection, food security, diet and non-communicable disease, schools and education, governance. A record belongs to every cluster any of its tokens hits, and is counted once per cluster. Tokens no rule matches are listed, not guessed: 51 of 457 policy tokens (2.9% of mentions), 9 of 124 programme tokens (8.1%), none of the 20 mechanism tokens. Every mechanism also counts as governance. The full token-to-cluster map is written next to the panel.

Dating. A policy is active from its start year to its end year inclusive; no end year means still active. A programme with a start year is treated the same way; the undated questionnaire rows titled "GNPR 2009-2010" or "GNPR 2016-2017" are placed in their survey window (the team's decision of 10 September); other undated rows are excluded and counted. Mechanisms are active from their start year onward. Of the rows in the 49 countries: 928 policies (408 with an end year, 520 open), 1,647 programmes used (206 dated with an end, 203 open, 1,238 placed by survey window; 109 excluded) and 96 mechanisms (35 excluded).

Three families per group. For each cluster, for all records, for each source file, and for anaemia and fortification crossed with each file: stock is the number of distinct records active in the year; logstock is log(1 + stock), so a country with twenty programmes no longer dominates; any is 1 from the first record onward and never returns to 0. Sixty columns in all, complete on the grid: zero is the literal reading of the registry, not an imputation.

Events. first_years() takes, per country, the earliest start year among dated records only, per cluster and per file; survey-window rows never date an event. That is where the first-anaemia-policy year of the event study comes from (46 of 49 countries have one; the Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea and South Sudan do not).

In

2,878 registry rows across three files; 47 rules.

Sample used

928 + 1,647 + 96 records used; groups = 11 clusters + all + 3 files + 6 cluster-by-file combinations.

Out

60 feat_gd_* columns on 1,274 rows; a token map (601 tokens with their clusters and mention counts); a first-years table (49 × 9) that seeds events.csv; a counts report that is printed into the readiness note.

Tests that pin it down. A hand recount of active anaemia policies for a chosen country-year must equal the column; any must be binary and absorbing; a positive stock must imply any; log-stock must equal log1p of stock; every token must appear in the map; the registry files and the rules must be manifest inputs.

Frame

The panel plus the derived columns the models need, so that no model derives anything on its own.

script 01 · 01_prep_frame.py → results/frame.csvThe modelling frame: 1,274 × 72The panel plus everything the models need derived in one place: logs, lags, leads, the gap, the flag, the index.

What is derived.

  • Log income for both series (the build stops if any income is non-positive).
  • Lags 2, 3 and 5 years of each of the eight exposures and of deworming coverage (27 columns), and a 3-year lead of each exposure (8 columns), by shifting within country.
  • The outcome one year earlier, for the lagged-outcome row.
  • The women-minus-men gap in severe food insecurity.
  • The survey flag as a 0/1 integer; the sub-region label.
  • The women's index: the mean z-score of anaemia in all women, anaemia in pregnant women, underweight and overweight, standardised on the 2000–2023 rows, higher meaning worse; it exists only where all four exist (1,127 rows), so it ends where the NCD-RisC series end.

What it records about the sample. The main estimation sample is every 2000–2023 row with an outcome, log constant income and the lag-3 dose: 994 country-years, 49 countries, 2003–2023. The dose averages 1.89 policies in force (median 1, maximum 11); its overall standard deviation is 2.12 and its within-country standard deviation 1.66, the unit the bounds are stated in. Five countries (Central African Republic, Eritrea, Equatorial Guinea, South Sudan, Seychelles) never change their dose inside the sample, so the within-country coefficient is identified from 44. The eight lag-3 exposures correlate up to 0.95 with each other, which is why they only ever enter one at a time.

Models

Seven scripts, one frame. Each section says what goes in, with its shape, and what comes out.

script 02 · 02_fe_model.py · the headline modelDose ladderOne regression, then 38 check rows and 12 sub-period rows that change one thing each; −0.21 pp per policy, interval spanning zero.

Everything the team reports is ordinary least squares on the country-year panel. There is no logit and, apart from one sidebar, no machine learning: the outcome is a prevalence in percentage points, so linear fits are the right tool. The main model is a two-way fixed-effects regression, which with a staggered, continuous treatment is a generalised difference-in-differences: each country is compared with itself before and after its policies, net of what happened to the whole region that year. The ladder re-runs that regression under different assumptions; the event studies are explicit difference-in-differences around adoption; the specification curve refits it 336 ways; the bounds say how small an effect could have hidden; the held-out fit asks whether the dose predicts; the context model is a cross-sectional regression used only to rank countries. Every model reads the same frame.

The main row. Anaemia in country i and year t is regressed on a country effect, a year effect, the number of dated anaemia-cluster policies in force in t−3, and log GDP per capita. The country effect removes each country's level (geography, health system, everything fixed); the year effect removes anything that moved the whole region in a year; the dose is lagged three years to allow implementation. Standard errors are clustered by country, and a wild cluster bootstrap-t (Rademacher weights, 999 draws, null imposed, on the fixed-effects-residualised regression) is reported beside every plain fixed-effects row because 49 clusters is few. Result: −0.209 pp per policy, 95% interval −0.623 to +0.204, p 0.32 (bootstrap p 0.33), two-way within-R² 0.023; per within-country standard deviation of the dose, −0.35 pp.

The rungs, and what each guards against.

  • Timing. Lag 2 (−0.25) and lag 5 (−0.16) instead of 3: the lag was not a lucky choice. The lead test puts the dose three years later on the right-hand side: a lead cannot cause today's anaemia, so if it predicts it the policies are following trends or targeting. It does: −0.34, interval −0.66 to −0.03, p 0.032 (bootstrap 0.040), on 1,088 rows; with the lag alongside, −0.27, p 0.043.
  • Dynamics. A linear trend per country absorbs slow drifts (and, as Wolfers 2006 warns, slowly phasing-in effects): −0.017. First differences (−0.013), five-year differences (−0.058) and a lagged-outcome model instead of country effects (−0.025; persistence 0.987) bracket the fixed-effects estimate under their respective assumptions.
  • Controls. Malaria, fertility and urbanisation together (−0.05, 931 rows), each alone, HIV, basic sanitation, unemployment and employment (the team's ask): −0.01 to −0.22, all spanning zero. Delivered deworming coverage as the exposure instead of the paper dose: +0.001 per point.
  • Exposure. Seven alternative constructions of the dose, one at a time: its log, the team's six-flag composite, the all-file stock and its log, ever-any-anaemia-policy, ever-any-fortification, the team's active-policy count; three of them also with country trends. The absorbing ever-adopted indicators are the fragile ones: they flip sign when trends are added, which is why the event study, not this row, is the check that holds.
  • Outcome. Pregnant-women anaemia, underweight, overweight, low birthweight and the women's index in place of the outcome, p-values adjusted across the five by Benjamini–Hochberg: all near zero, adjusted p 0.80.
  • Sample. Income in current dollars (−0.15); without the two thinnest income series, Eritrea and South Sudan (−0.21); only survey-anchored country-years, 80 rows in 33 countries (−0.04, p 0.90); and six sub-period pairs where a control exists only for some years (food insecurity and the gender gap from 2015, unaffordability from 2017, deworming from 2005, hospital beds and health spending on their own rows), each shown next to the base specification on the same rows.

Bounds. A null is only informative with a bound. The smallest effect of interest was fixed in advance at 1 pp of anaemia per within-country standard deviation of the dose (0.60 pp per policy). The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is 0.59 pp per policy (0.98 per within-SD). The 90% interval per policy runs from −0.556 to +0.137, and the two one-sided tests reject an effect as large as the smallest one of interest (p 0.031). Reading: benefits larger than about 0.9 pp per within-SD step of the dose are ruled out; anything smaller could not have been detected by this design.

script 02b · 02b_event_study.py · difference-in-differencesEvent studiesWhat happens around a country's first anaemia policy and around its wheat-flour mandate, under three estimators built for staggered adoption.

Why an event study at all. The ladder asks about a dose; the event study asks what happens in the years around an adoption, which is the sharper test of timing. With adoption staggered across countries, the ordinary two-way fixed-effects estimator mixes comparisons it should not make (already-treated countries serve as controls for later adopters), so two estimators designed for that situation run beside it.

Building the frame. For each country, g is the event year: the first dated anaemia-cluster policy in the policies file, or the year wheat-flour fortification became mandatory. Event time is year − g, binned into nine dummies from four-or-more years before to five-or-more after, with the year before adoption as the reference. Countries that adopted before 2002 have no usable pre-period and are dropped (15 for the anaemia policy, named in the report); a country whose event falls after 2023 is never treated inside the window; never-adopters and not-yet-adopters are the comparison group.

The three estimators.

  1. Two-way fixed effects (pyfixest): the outcome on the nine bins and log income with country and year effects, errors clustered by country; plus a static version on a single post indicator whose weights are decomposed the de Chaisemartin–D'Haultfœuille way. For the anaemia policy 27% of that coefficient's weight is negative (135 of 451 treated cells), so it is shown only next to the estimators that correct it.
  2. Two-stage difference-in-differences (Gardner 2022, implemented directly): stage one fits country effects, year effects and log income on the untreated observations only and residualises every row; stage two regresses that residual on the nine bins (and, for the average effect, on post). Standard errors come from a country cluster bootstrap of 299 draws in which both stages are re-estimated, so the first-stage uncertainty is carried.
  3. Local-projections difference-in-differences (Dube, Girardi, Jordà and Taylor 2023, pyfixest): for each horizon a separate regression of the change in anaemia from the year before adoption to that horizon on the treatment switch, using only clean controls; the horizons are then pooled into one average effect.

Results. First anaemia policy: naive two-way FE −0.94 pp (p 0.08) with a pre-trend of the same sign (−0.11 pp per year; trend-adjusted post mean −0.33); two-stage DiD +0.10 (interval −2.04 to +2.09); local projections −0.08 (−0.47 to +0.31). Wheat-flour mandate: −0.56, −1.05 and −0.17, every interval spanning zero, no pre-trend and no break. The event study, not the country-trend row, is the decisive dynamic check, because unit trends absorb effects that phase in slowly.

script 03 · 03_loco.py · out-of-sampleHeld-out fit (leave one country out)Does knowing a country's policy count help predict its anaemia path when the model never saw that country? No.

How it runs. One common sample is fixed first: the 994 rows that have the outcome, log income and all eight lag-3 exposures, so every model is scored on identical rows. For each of the 49 countries the model is fitted by ordinary least squares on the other 48 (country dummies, year dummies, the regressors), and the held-out country's anaemia is predicted as deviations from its own mean: each regressor's coefficient times the regressor's deviation from its within-country mean, plus the year effects centred. The observed deviation is anaemia minus the country's mean. Errors are pooled over all 994 held-out predictions. Five nested models are compared with a naive baseline that predicts no deviation at all: year effects only, plus income, plus the dose, plus the team composite instead of the dose, plus all eight exposures. A context ladder repeats the exercise on the 931 rows (46 countries) that also have malaria, fertility and urbanisation. A bootstrap over countries (500 resamples) gives an interval for the change in error when the dose is added.

script 09 · 09_dml_sidebar.py · sidebar, the one machine-learning pieceDouble machine learningThe partially linear model with random-forest nuisance functions; a same-sign check the team asked for, nothing more.

Finding 1

Where it is most concerning

Finding 2

The policy record does not move anaemia, measurably

One more policy in force is followed three years later by −0.21 points, interval −0.62 to +0.20, and the estimate stays at zero across lags, controls, estimators and 336 specifications. Context explains 81% of the differences in levels.
